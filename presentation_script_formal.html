2.0 Background & Problem Statement

Public Utility Jeepneys (PUJs) remain the backbone of short-range public transport along the 5.2-kilometer corridor connecting the Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Muntinlupa (PLMun) campus in Barangay Poblacion to the commercial and multimodal transport terminals in Alabang. Despite their critical importance, commuters, drivers, and transport operators face three persistent systemic challenges:

  • Unpredictable Commuter Waiting Times: Students and local passengers have no mechanism to know when the next vehicle will arrive at designated stops, causing congestion at curb stops and excessive delays during morning and evening rush hours.
  • Unknown Seat Capacity: Passengers frequently wait for approaching jeepneys only to discover upon arrival that the vehicle is completely full, leading to missed trips and dangerous roadside boarding attempts.
  • Lack of Real-Time Dispatch Oversight: Transport cooperatives and local traffic managers operate without real-time tracking data, preventing automated terminal bay management, headway regulation (preventing vehicle bunching), and immediate response to vehicle breakdowns.

Project Objective: JEEPTRACK addresses these gaps by establishing a bidirectional, low-latency tracking and operations platform that synchronizes commuters, onboard drivers, and municipal dispatchers through standard web standards.

4.0 Technical Glossary & Plain-English Explanations

This section provides concise, formal definitions for technical terms utilized within the JEEPTRACK system architecture.

Technical Term Formal Definition & Mechanism Operational Purpose in JEEPTRACK
GPS Telemetry The continuous electronic transmission of geospatial data packets comprising latitude, longitude, instantaneous speed, and bearing angle from onboard sensors to server endpoints. Enables real-time rendering of moving vehicle markers and trajectory calculation on the corridor map.
Geofencing A location-based technology that defines a virtual spatial boundary using geographic coordinates and a predetermined radial distance (e.g., 80 meters). Automatically registers vehicle arrival, bay occupancy, and departure at PLMun and Alabang terminals without manual clerical input.
Dynamic ETA (Estimated Time of Arrival) An algorithmic arrival calculation based on remaining route distance divided by real-time corridor velocity, updated dynamically as road conditions vary. Provides commuters with realistic arrival countdowns that account for localized traffic delays.
Corridor Path Interpolation Mathematical projection of coordinates along a predefined polyline trajectory corresponding to the Manila South Road transit geometry. Ensures accurate visual mapping and provides continuous tracking simulation during academic evaluation and testing.
Headway Management The temporal or spatial interval between successive public transit vehicles traversing the same route and direction. Assists dispatchers in preventing 'vehicle bunching' (multiple empty units arriving simultaneously followed by long gaps).
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) An information security mechanism restricting system features and database operations based on verified user authorization (Commuter, Driver, Operator). Protects fare matrices, dispatch controls, and administrative logs from unauthorized access or alteration.

5.0 Committee Evaluation & Defense Q&A

Q1: How does the system handle temporary cellular data loss or connectivity dropouts along the corridor?
Answer: The system maintains state resilience by recording the last confirmed GPS timestamp and location. If a vehicle misses consecutive heartbeat pings, its status transitions to 'GPS Standby / Offline' with an explicit indicator (e.g., 'Last seen 3 minutes ago'). When mobile data reconnects, the telemetry pipeline re-establishes synchronization automatically. Central dispatch is immediately alerted if an active vehicle unexpectedly stops transmitting along an active route.
Q2: Can the platform support traditional Public Utility Jeepneys that lack built-in electronic control units?
Answer: Yes. JEEPTRACK was intentionally engineered to avoid reliance on expensive proprietary hardware. Traditional PUJ operators only require an entry-level smartphone or tablet mounted on the dashboard running the driver web application. Modern Euro-4 and Electric PUJs can utilize the exact same interface or connect via onboard GPS telemetry hardware.
Q3: How are student and senior citizen discount fares verified to prevent fraudulent fare calculations?
Answer: The Commuter Portal features an accredited digital transit profile displaying the verified student number, institutional identification, and photo badge. Commuters present this digital credential upon boarding, allowing drivers to confirm eligibility for the automated 20% legal discount computed by the fare matrix.
Q4: What was the architectural rationale for developing a Progressive Web Application (PWA) rather than native mobile applications?
Answer: A high-performance web architecture eliminates client-side installation friction, app store approval delays, and smartphone storage constraints for students and drivers. The platform runs across Android, iOS, Windows, and macOS devices through any standard compliant browser while preserving high-performance vector mapping and responsive touch interaction.
